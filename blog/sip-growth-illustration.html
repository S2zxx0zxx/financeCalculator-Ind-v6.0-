How a monthly savings illustration works

Understand how regular month-end contributions add up under an assumed constant return—and why that is not a forecast.

Savings mathFinCalc EditorialPublished 27 Sep 2026

Regular contributions in a formula

A monthly savings illustration treats each contribution as a deposit at a particular time. With a constant assumed monthly return, earlier contributions have more periods to grow than later ones. Real investment returns change over time and may be negative; they do not follow a smooth line.

The future-value formula

FV = C × (((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r)

C is the monthly contribution, r the assumed monthly rate, and n the number of contributions. If r = 0, future value is simply C × n. This tool uses nominal annual return divided by 12 for its month-end illustration.

Worked example

₹5,000 deposited at each month end for 10 years gives 120 contributions, or ₹6,00,000 invested.

At a constant assumed 10% nominal annual return, the formula gives about ₹10,24,224.89. That is a hypothetical mathematical outcome, not an expected or promised gain.

Adjust the assumptions in the monthly investment illustration.

What the calculation leaves out

It does not model market volatility, fees, taxes, inflation, missed contributions or a specific mutual fund. A steady positive rate can make an investment path look far more predictable than it is. Use it only to understand how assumptions affect the arithmetic.

Scope: Formula explanation only. This is not a live rate, forecast, product offer, filing guide or personalized recommendation.

Example assumptions

₹5,000 at month end · 120 deposits · assumed 10% nominal annual return divided monthly. No fees, tax or volatility.
