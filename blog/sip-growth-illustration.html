How a monthly savings illustration works

Understand the future-value formula for regular month-end contributions without treating a return assumption as a forecast.

A recurring monthly contribution can be illustrated as a series of deposits. If an assumed monthly return is constant, earlier deposits have more months to grow than later deposits. Actual investment returns are not constant and are not guaranteed.

The formula used

For contribution C, monthly rate r and n month-end deposits, the illustrative future value is C × (((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r). When the assumed rate is zero, it is simply C × n. This page uses nominal annual return divided by 12 for the monthly illustration.

Worked example

₹5,000 at each month end for 10 years means 120 contributions and ₹6,00,000 deposited. At an assumed constant 10% nominal annual return, this formula gives about ₹10,24,224.89. The difference from contributions is a mathematical scenario, not a promised gain.

Change the inputs in the SIP illustration and read its assumptions before interpreting the output.

Limits

The model excludes volatility, fees, taxes, inflation, skipped contributions and the timing differences in real products. A steady positive return assumption can make outcomes look smoother than real investments. Compare scenarios as arithmetic only, not as personalized investment advice.

Scope: This guide explains the formula used by FinCalc, not a bank offer, investment forecast, tax opinion or personalized recommendation.
