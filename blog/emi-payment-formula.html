How fixed-rate EMI is calculated

A transparent derivation of the standard fixed-payment loan formula, with a reproducible example and its limits.

A fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan divides repayment into equal monthly payments. Each payment covers interest due for that month and reduces some principal. As the outstanding balance falls, the interest portion usually falls too.

The formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P is principal, r is the monthly interest rate as a decimal, and n is the number of monthly payments. For a quoted nominal annual percentage rate, this illustration uses r = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100.

Worked example

For ₹10,00,000 at 8.5% nominal annual interest over 20 years: r = 0.085 ÷ 12 and n = 240. The formula gives a monthly payment of about ₹8,678.23, total payments of about ₹20,82,775.76 and interest of about ₹10,82,775.76 before fees or rounding.

You can reproduce it in the EMI calculator by entering those three values.

What the estimate leaves out

This arithmetic assumes the rate stays fixed, payments happen monthly, and the lender uses the same rounding convention. It does not include fees, insurance, taxes, variable-rate resets, missed payments or prepayments. A lender’s repayment schedule can differ.
