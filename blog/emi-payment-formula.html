How fixed-rate EMI is calculated

See how a loan’s principal, monthly rate and payment count combine in the standard fixed-payment formula.

EMI mathFinCalc EditorialPublished 27 Sep 2026

What a fixed EMI means

A fixed-rate, fully amortizing loan uses an equal scheduled payment each month. Every payment first covers that period’s interest; the remainder reduces principal. Since the balance shrinks over time, the interest portion of the payment generally falls while the principal portion grows.

The formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P is the starting principal, r the monthly rate as a decimal, and n the number of monthly payments. For this illustration, a nominal annual rate is divided by 12 and by 100 to get r.

Worked example

₹10,00,000 at 8.5% nominal annual interest for 20 years means r = 0.085 ÷ 12 and n = 240.

Estimated monthly payment: ₹8,678.23. Total of 240 payments: about ₹20,82,775.76; interest portion: about ₹10,82,775.76, before fees and lender rounding.

Enter those values in the EMI calculator to reproduce the estimate.

Where an actual schedule can differ

This formula assumes the rate stays fixed and payments are made monthly. It does not model fees, insurance, taxes, missed payments, prepayments or rate resets. Lenders may use different day counts and rounding. Check the sanction letter and repayment schedule for the actual amount.

Scope: Formula explanation only. This is not a live rate, forecast, product offer, filing guide or personalized recommendation.

Example assumptions

Principal ₹10,00,000 · 8.5% nominal annual rate · 240 month-end payments. Fees and rate changes are excluded.
