How compounding changes a balance

When interest is added to the balance, later interest can apply to that larger amount. A fixed nominal annual rate compounded at regular intervals can be illustrated with this equation:

A = P × (1 + i ÷ m)m × t

P is the starting amount, i the annual rate as a decimal, m the number of compounding periods per year, and t the number of years.

Worked example

₹1,00,000 at an assumed annual rate of 7%, compounded quarterly for 3 years: 100000 × (1 + 0.07 ÷ 4)12.

Illustrated maturity: about ₹1,23,143.93; interest before tax: about ₹23,143.93.

Check the product terms

Real deposit products can differ in compounding, payout timing, eligibility, tax withholding and premature closure rules. Review the institution’s current terms. This equation does not compare providers, determine deposit protection or say whether a particular deposit suits you.

Scope: Formula explanation only. This is not a live rate, forecast, product offer, filing guide or personalized recommendation.

Example assumptions

₹1,00,000 · assumed 7% annual rate · quarterly compounding · 3 years · before tax and product-specific terms.
