How compound interest illustrations work

See the compound-interest equation and why an assumed rate is not a bank quote or maturity promise.

Compound interest adds earned interest to a balance, so later interest can apply to the enlarged balance. A basic illustration with a fixed annual nominal rate and regular compounding is:

A = P × (1 + i ÷ m)m × t

P is starting principal, i is the annual rate as a decimal, m is compounding periods per year and t is years.

Worked example

₹1,00,000 at an assumed 7% annual rate compounded quarterly for 3 years gives 100000 × (1 + 0.07 ÷ 4)12, or about ₹1,23,143.93 before tax. The illustrated interest is about ₹23,143.93.

Limits

Real deposits can use product-specific compounding, payout, eligibility, tax withholding and premature closure rules. Verify terms with the deposit-taking institution. This formula does not assess deposit insurance limits, suitability or the safety of a particular bank.
